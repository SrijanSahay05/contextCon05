## **The theme: Services, Replaced.** 

Services are at a turning point. For twenty years, software helped recruiters, analysts and diligence firms do their jobs. Now itʼs starting to do the jobs itself. 

Companies spend far more on services than on software. Recruiting firms, auditors, insurance brokers and freight forwarders are paid for an outcome, not a tool, and agents can now deliver it. The latest models finish a full dayʼs work on their own, act through email, calls and payments, and look up who someone is and what a company does in one canonical graph. 

Building a service is different from building a tool: parts delivered rather than suppliers listed, invoices paid rather than invoices read, fraud caught rather than flagged, offers signed rather than candidates sourced. 

Pick a job people do today, in-house or at a firm, from hiring to underwriting to freight, and build the agents that do all of it. Bring in a human only for the exceptions, make every decision cite its reasons, and do it all in seven hours and forty-five minutes.

- An underwriter that checks every submission against the world, then quotes, refers or declines 

- A scout that finds exceptional founders before they raise, and says why each one deserves a meeting 

- A fraud investigator that takes an insider tip and returns a complaint-ready case file

## **Annexure B. The themes in full** 

Agents that turn a whole service into software. Build inside a theme, or bring your own idea: any service people are paid to do is fair game. 

##### **1. Hiring** 

**Paid today:** Search firms, for each role they fill, and background-check firms, for each candidate they verify. 

Recruiting firms earn their fee when a person starts the job; a stack of profiles earns nothing. The right engineer calls the job something else, changed roles last month and shares a name with two strangers. 

**Build an agent that fills roles with no one at the keyboard:** 

- **1.1 The Search Desk:** From a managerʼs three lines to a first call, tested on real hires. 

- **1.2 The Career Check:** Every line of a CV checked against the record.

Weʼre looking for shortlists that hold the people companies really hired, built only from what the record showed before each hire. 

##### **2. Investing** 

**Paid today:** Venture scouts and outside diligence firms, for the companies they surface and the claims they check. 

A fundʼs analysts hunt for companies early, test what founders claim and watch every company in the portfolio. Every call rests on what was public that day, and the answer arrives years later. 

**Build an agent that makes a fundʼs calls on its own:** 

- **2.1 The Pre-Round Scout:** Who raises next, replayed from a past date. 

- **2.2 The Diligence Desk:** Every pitch-deck claim tested before the second meeting. 

Weʼre looking for calls replayed from a past date that match what happened next, with no fact from after that date. 

##### **3. Research and markets** 

**Paid today:** Market research and strategy consulting firms, for each market map or answer they deliver. Research firms and strategy teams answer questions about markets for clients who act on the answer. The reading never ends, and a model answers from old memory with full confidence. 

**Build an agent that gets to the answer before the market does:** 

- **3.1 The Market Map:** Every company in a market, once, with a cited reason. 

- **3.2 The Research Desk:** Dated, sourced answers, or the false premise named. 

Weʼre looking for answers about events no model has seen, each number traced to a dated source, where a wrong answer costs more than none. 

##### **4. Insurance** 

**Paid today:** Brokers, through a commission on each policy they place, and insurers, through the premium on each one they write. 

Underwriters price a business from the answers on its application, and the applicant writes those answers. Forms understate size and leave out risky work, and checking each one by hand takes days. 

###### **Build an agent that insures a business with no one in the loop:** 

- **4.1 The Underwriting Desk:** Quote, refer or decline, checked against the record. 

Weʼre looking for the same decision however the applicant words the form, with a source behind every reason. 

##### **5. Trust and compliance** 

**Paid today:** Verification and screening firms, for each business or person they check for a bank, a payment firm or a large buyer.

Compliance analysts decide whether a business or a person is who they claim to be, before money moves. Records share no common key, a slow source looks like a missing one, and a front company can carry genuine papers. 

###### **Build an agent that decides who to trust:** 

- **5.1 The Vendor Check:** A new vendor proven real, active and run by its director.

##### **6. Buying and paying** 

**Paid today:** Outsourced accounting firms, for running a companyʼs payables and closing its books each month. 

Payables teams stand between an invoice and the companyʼs bank account. Duplicates slip through in new formats, and the costliest fraud is a polite email asking to change bank details.

- **6.1 The Payables Desk:** Each invoice paid once, through crashes and bank-detail fraud.

**Paid today:** Research operations teams, for buying the data a clientʼs job needs, inside its budget.

##### **8. Supply chain** 

**Paid today:** Sourcing agents, through a commission on each order they place with a factory. 

Sourcing teams find manufacturers, check they are real and able to deliver, and run quotes by email and spreadsheet. Traders pose as factories, certificates lapse, and a promise of four weeks means eight. 

###### **Build an agent that runs freight and parts operations end to end:** 

- **8.1 The Sourcing Desk:** Real makers found, quotes run, an order recommended. 

Weʼre looking for parts made by real factories at the quoted price and date, with every record agreeing.

##### **9. Revenue** 

**Paid today:** Sales development agencies and lead qualification firms, for each meeting they book with the right buyer.

Weʼre looking for parts that keep their promises through a crash, a change of model and a poisoned input.
